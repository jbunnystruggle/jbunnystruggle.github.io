EdSafe AI Alliance

Parent AI Literacy Research & Framework

2025 Women in AI Fellow · 2025–2026

13% of parents received a clear AI policy from their school
74% believe parents and schools should be equally responsible for teaching kids about AI

Selected for a global cohort of 30 for the 2025 EdSafe AI Alliance - Women in AI Fellowship. Leading a solo mixed-methods capstone on parent AI literacy — addressing what I called "the parent-shaped hole" in K-12 AI education.

  • Conducted 80-respondent survey and 8 in-depth interviews — key findings: 74% of parents believe schools and families are equally responsible for teaching kids about AI, yet only 13% received a clear AI policy from their school and 48% don't know if their kids use AI for schoolwork

  • Publishing a practitioner framework for district leaders and edtech executives on parent AI literacy, child-specific risk/benefit profiles, and implementation pathways

CraftYourImpact

Solo-Built AI Performance Review Tool

Founder · 2026

4-step LLM pipeline
~32% cost reduction via model routing

Stack

Claude / Anthropic API Claude Code Cloudflare Workers Supabase PostHog GitHub

Built end-to-end solo — product, design, and technical direction. Transforms scattered work documentation into polished performance review narratives. Launched in beta June 2026.

  • Designed a 4-step LLM pipeline (extraction → synthesis → bias check → gap analysis); diagnosed Claude Code defaulting every API call to Sonnet and rebuilt as three-model routing — ~32% cost reduction, eliminated rate limit issues, no loss in output quality
  • Built the full production stack solo — Cloudflare Worker API proxy, Supabase, PostHog observability, and DEI-aware bias detection via JSON patch
  • AI defaults to the most efficient path, not the most thorough one, and it won't flag that it's doing it. Every scoping, review, and governance decision in the product is built around that reality.

About

10+ years in marketing. 4 years building.

I started in creative advertising agencies learning brand from the people who actually built it: art directors, copywriters, strategists. Leading work for Nike Women, MINI Cooper, and Kennedy Space Center taught me how to create value and emotional connection through brand and product marketing. That work put me inside every stage of a product's market life: building its identity, seeding awareness, finding the right channel mix, and scaling what worked.

The second half of those years looked different. At MKG, a B2B SaaS digital marketing agency, I joined as the first account director hire and eventually became Managing Director. I built out the client service model and project processes from scratch, and the work itself was data-heavy: SEO, analytics, and PPC for tech companies ranging from growth-stage startups to enterprise clients like Cloudera, Informatica, and ExtraHop. That's where the analytical practice really sharpened.

Between the two I learned when to lead with narrative and when to lead with data, and how to hold your position when the pressure goes up. Those same skills show up in every engineering sprint review, partner negotiation, and executive presentation.

At Discovery Education, I became their first Assessment PM, then first AI/ML PM. Writing the AI policy without guidelines, leading a 200-school beta, working directly with data science on model evaluation, and coordinating across legal, privacy, and GTM to ship.

I also build things on my own. CraftYourImpact is a solo-built AI performance review tool with a full production stack, launched in beta in June 2026. Building it deepened my practice: knowing when to trust the tooling and when to slow down and reason through it myself.

I'm also the fractional PM for AI-Powered Women, where I built a C-suite and entrepreneur-focused AI certification program — curriculum, instructional design, and LMS architecture — solo for their MIT-based conference. And a 2025 EdSafe AI Fellow completing a mixed-methods research project on parent AI literacy in K-12: what schools aren't communicating to families, and what a practical response looks like. Both are current.

I'm based in Williamstown, MA, available for remote work. On Substack I write about AI: the tradeoffs, failure modes, and what's worth slowing down for.
