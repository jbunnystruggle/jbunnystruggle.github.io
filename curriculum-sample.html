Vibe Coding · Section 4

When You're Ready to Commit

The shift from prototype to production — and the four decisions that belong before the first prompt is written.

The Shift

What Changes When You Commit

You've validated the concept, aligned your stakeholders, and learned what your users actually need. Now the work changes. You're no longer optimizing for speed of insight. You're optimizing for reliability, maintainability, and the experience of someone who will use this repeatedly, without you present, and expect it to work.

Prototype Production
Built to generate learning Built to be depended on
Approximate, exploratory, conversational Specified, intentional, precise
Optimized for speed of insight Optimized for reliability and maintainability

The same conversation-first approach — but the standard shifts. That shift has implications for how you prompt, how you review, and how you govern what gets built.

Before the First Prompt

Four Decisions That Belong at the Start

These decisions belong at the start of a production build, not at the end. Make them before the first prompt is written.

1

What's the cost if version one is imperfect?

A product tied to an established brand reputation has a different standard than an early-stage startup. Name that standard explicitly before you start — it shapes every review decision that follows.
2

Is this replacing something or creating something new?

A build that replaces an existing system carries migration risk — existing users, existing data, existing expectations. Know which you're doing. The prompting approach, the rollout plan, and the review criteria are different for each.
3

Who owns it after it's built?

Name the owner before the first line of code is written. Not the team — a specific person for each element. Who updates it when the underlying workflow changes? Who fields questions when something breaks? An ownerless production build becomes a liability faster than a useful one, regardless of how well it was built.
4

What does done look like for this phase?

Define the scope of version one explicitly — what it does, what it deliberately doesn't do yet, and what standard it needs to meet before it goes live. Write it down. This is your governance document and your review checklist. Everything gets measured against it.
Using What You Already Have

From Prototype to Build

Everything you learned in the prototype phase is now an asset you bring directly into the build. You're not starting from scratch — you're already in progress. There are three ways to carry your prototype forward.

📸
Method 1
Share Screenshots

Most vibe coding tools accept images as input. Take screenshots of your working prototype — the key screens, the core flow, the interactions that tested well — and bring them into your production build session. The tool sees what you've validated and builds toward it rather than interpreting a description of it.

Here are screenshots of a prototype I've validated with [users / stakeholders].

The core flow that worked: [describe what the screenshots show]

What I need the production version to improve on:
– [Specific improvement 1]
– [Specific improvement 2]

Build the production version starting with [core screen / function], matching the layout and flow from the screenshots unless I specify otherwise.
💻
Method 2
Export the Code

If your prototype was built in a tool that allows code export, export it and bring that code into your production build session as the starting point. You're not starting over — you're elevating what already works to a production standard.

Please export the complete code for what we've built. I need all files — HTML, CSS, JavaScript — so I can bring this into a production environment. Also provide a summary of the main components, any dependencies, and any known prototype-quality limitations.
💬
Method 3
Carry the Context

If you can't export the code, document what you built: the core user flows, the key decisions you made, the feedback you received, and what you'd do differently. Bring that summary into your production session. The detail you captured during prototyping is now your spec.

I prototyped [product name] to test [core hypothesis]. Here's what I learned:

What worked: [specific flows, features, design decisions]
What didn't: [pain points, confusion, friction]
User feedback: [key quotes or patterns]

Build the production version incorporating these lessons, starting with [highest-priority element].

AI Leadership Playbook · Module 3

Strategic Prompting Guide: From Basic Queries to Executive-Level Analysis

Transforming AI from a productivity tool into a strategic thinking partner for leadership, analysis, and decision-making.

The Foundation

Architecting a Strategic Prompt

A basic prompt gets a generic answer. A strategic prompt generates a bespoke analysis that reflects your leadership intelligence. Every powerful prompt is built on five core pillars.

1

Context

Provide the essential background. What history, data, or goals must the AI understand to deliver a relevant response?

2

Task

State the specific action you need the AI to perform. Use strong verbs like analyze, synthesize, or develop.

3

Persona

Define the expert lens the AI should adopt — e.g., a CFO, Chief Risk Officer, or Head of Customer Experience.

4

Constraints

Define the guardrails. What is the desired tone or length? What should be included or excluded?

5

Format

Specify the output structure. Should it be a bulleted list, a memo, a table, or a structured report?

Advanced Techniques

From Analysis to Competitive Advantage

Master these techniques to transform AI from a simple tool into a sophisticated thought partner.

1. Multi-Stakeholder Scenario Analysis

Why it works: It surfaces the hidden costs and opportunities of any decision by modeling its impact on the entire ecosystem — customers, team members, investors, community. It moves beyond a pro/con list to a rich, multi-dimensional analysis.

Adaptable Template
"Analyze the strategic decision to [implement a new AI-powered sales tool] through a multi-stakeholder lens.

Customer Perspective: How does this impact our most complex, high-value customers versus our transactional ones? Where could it create frustration or delight?
Team Perspective: How does this change the most meaningful parts of our sales team's work? What new skills are required, and how can we support that development?
Financial Perspective: Beyond the initial ROI, what are the long-term impacts on customer lifetime value and brand equity?
Ethical Perspective: Where could this system introduce bias in lead scoring or customer interaction? What safeguards must we build in from the start?

Synthesize these viewpoints into a balanced recommendation that optimizes for sustainable growth, not just short-term sales metrics."

2. Self-Criticism Prompting

Why it works: It forces the AI to stress-test its own conclusions, identify weak points in its logic, and consider alternatives — helping you anticipate objections and build a more resilient strategy.

Template 1 — Stress-Testing a Strategic Plan
"Part 1: Develop a strategic plan for [entering the European market] based on this data: [paste market research data].
Part 2: Now, critically evaluate your own analysis.
  • What are the three strongest counterarguments to this plan?
  • What critical assumptions have you made about [regulatory changes or consumer behavior]?
  • What crucial data points are missing that would change your recommendation?
  • What alternative, lower-risk strategy could achieve 70% of the benefit?
Part 3: Revise your initial strategic plan based on this self-criticism. Present the final recommendation, explicitly highlighting how it now mitigates the risks you identified."
Template 2 — Gaining Critical Feedback on a Presentation
"Part 1: I am presenting to [the executive board]. My primary objective is to [secure funding for a new R&D initiative]. The three key ideas I must communicate are [1. the significant market gap, 2. our unique approach, 3. projected 5-year ROI].
Part 2: Act as a skeptical but fair board member. Critically review the attached presentation.
  • Where is my argument least convincing?
  • Which slide is most likely to cause confusion?
  • What tough question am I not anticipating?
Part 3: Provide specific, actionable recommendations for strengthening the presentation. Suggest changes to slide titles, data visualizations, or narrative structure to make it more persuasive."

AI Leadership Playbook · Module 5

Strategic Guide: Creating Gold Star Data

How leaders guide the creation of high-quality training data that makes AI systems more accurate, more aligned, and more differentiated.

The Strategic Framing

Your Most Defensible Competitive Moat in the AI Age

In the rush to adopt AI, the most common strategic error is focusing on the quantity of data instead of its quality. Many leaders feel daunted, believing they can't compete with the massive, internet-scale datasets used to train Large Language Models. This is a misconception that leads to flawed strategy.

While public LLMs are trained on the entire internet (massive quantity) to learn language, your proprietary AI must be trained on your best thinking — massive quality.

The real leadership question isn't "How much data can we feed the AI?" — it's "How do we use our deep human intelligence to curate a dataset that teaches the AI to think like our best people?"

What Is It?

The Four Attributes of Gold Star Data

Gold Star data is a dataset meticulously vetted, labeled, and validated by your top human experts to serve as the definitive source of truth for training and testing an AI model. Think of it as the AI's answer key — if the answer key is wrong, the AI will learn the wrong answer and scale that mistake with perfect efficiency and confidence.

Accurate & Correct

The data is factually right. If a customer ticket is about a billing error, it is labeled "Billing Error," not "General Inquiry." Precision at the labeling stage determines reliability at scale.

Values-Aligned & Nuanced

This is where "correct" becomes "correct for us." A generic AI labels an email "Angry." Your Gold Star data, labeled by your best agent, specifies: "Frustrated-High-Value" vs. "High-Risk-Churn." That nuance is your competitive edge.

Representative & Comprehensive

The dataset must reflect your entire user base, not just the most common cases. This means intentionally including the difficult edge cases — the non-native speakers, the complex bug reports, the unusual requests — that generic models fail to handle.

Audited for Bias

The data has been systematically reviewed to ensure it doesn't perpetuate harmful stereotypes or systemic biases. If historical data consistently rates requests from certain groups as "less urgent," a Gold Star dataset corrects this with expert re-labeling.

The Framework

How to Create Your Gold Star Data: A 5-Step Process

The tactical process for turning messy raw data into a high-impact strategic asset.

1
Define Your Human-Centered Goal

Before you touch a single spreadsheet, define what "success" means in human terms. This defines what you will label.

Weak goal (efficiency-based): "Reduce customer service response time."

"Increase customer satisfaction among our most complex-case customers by 20%, by ensuring they are routed to a human expert within 3 minutes." — A strong, human-centered goal that defines precisely what to label.
2
Assemble Your Human Intelligence Team

Your best human experts — not AI — do the labeling. Identify who in your organization has the deepest judgment on the outcome you're optimizing for. These people become your "annotators." Their expertise is what makes the dataset proprietary.

3
Curate, Label, and Build Consensus

Take your raw data and have your expert team "grade" it based on your Step 1 goal. Here is what that transformation looks like in practice:

Before — raw system data (data without judgment):

Ticket_IDStatusRaw_Text
45A-01Open"this is the 3rd time i'm writing. my bill is wrong again. i'm so tired of this. just cancel it."
45A-02Open"hi, where can i find my last invoice? thanks"

After — Gold Star expert-labeled data (data with judgment):

Ticket_IDTrue_IntentSentimentRisk_LevelPriority
45A-01Billing_Error_RecurringAngryHigh_Churn_RiskUrgent_Human_Escalation
45A-02Info_Request_InvoiceNeutralLowLow_Auto_Reply

Tactical rule: For your most critical labels, have the same data reviewed by at least two — ideally three — expert annotators. Where they disagree, convene a calibration session. Disagreement is signal: it means your labeling criteria aren't precise enough yet.

4
Conduct a Strategic Bias Audit

Review the Gold Star set you've created and ask the hard questions using bias-navigation intelligence:

→ "Does our set of High_Churn_Risk examples only include customers who use direct or strong language? Are we missing customers who 'quietly quit'?"

→ "Do our Gold Star examples all come from one team, one region, or one demographic?"

→ "What intersectional perspectives are we missing? How will this AI handle a customer who is older, a non-native English speaker, and using a mobile device?"

Use this audit to identify gaps, then actively find or create data examples from under-represented groups. This is what makes your AI robust, fair, and more effective than competitors'.

5
Start Small to Validate and Gain Buy-In

Before investing months labeling your entire data pool, run a strategic pilot on a small, high-impact slice. For customer service: start with one complaint category like "Billing Errors." For edtech: start with one subject area like "3rd Grade Math."

This allows your team to test and refine labeling rules and discover ambiguities before you've wasted time at scale. Then run the new Gold Star subset against your existing model — and go to leadership with a tangible A/B comparison, not a theoretical case.

"The model trained on our old data had a 40% error rate on 'Billing Errors.' The model fine-tuned on our Gold Star data has an 8% error rate. This validates the investment and proves we are ready to scale." — Your leadership pitch after Step 5.
